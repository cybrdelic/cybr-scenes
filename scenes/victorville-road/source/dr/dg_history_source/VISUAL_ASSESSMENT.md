# Actual visual assessment

The 500 x 400 native close comparison visibly reduces the blanket of high dark grit crowns. Historical cover embeds some existing grains while the retained deposited fraction stays exposed. Fine grains remain visible. The central coarse population still shows overly broad planar/cuboid fracture faces; this revision does not change those bodies.

The diagnostic uses the same source IDs, counts, metric sizes, horizontal placements, material response, camera and 22-degree daylight. It is 32 raw Cycles CPU samples with no denoising, resize or grade. Native source PNGs are 16-bit RGB; comparison layouts use an 8-bit display copy without rescaling the panels.

The grid is 5 cm display-allocation quadrature. Available sediment classes are reconciled by debiting later erosion against earlier retained deposition first. That bounded assumption is not tracked stratigraphy or validated physical transport. Fine sand instances coexist with a statistical microbed representation; they are not a disjoint enumeration of all physical grains.

The narrower DF control affected only 0.224% of whole-frame pixels and mostly a side region. DG addresses the older cover that occupies a much larger visible domain, but a percentage change is not a claim of photorealism. Grain dimensions are not enlarged to force visibility in distant pixels.

The actual 2000 x 1334 whole-road counterpart shows a more restrained, patchier fine-grain exposure, but the composition-level change is modest. Large angular foreground fragments, sparse plant architecture and the smooth original distant mountains remain visible limitations. Original mountain shapes are preserved at the user's request. The native whole-frame crop is 900 x 550 pixels with exact source-pixel equality.
